# Plain writing

Use this when the owner asks you to draft or rewrite something another person will read.
It is not about your chat replies, and a note in memex follows `memex-writing` when that is
in your skill list.

## Rules

1. **Lead with the point.** The first sentence says what the reader needs: the answer, the
   request, the decision, the date. No warm-up ("I hope this finds you well", "I wanted to
   reach out").
2. **Say it once.** No closing summary of what was just said, no "In short" or "To sum up",
   no "Let me know if you have any questions" unless the owner asked for it.
3. **Plain words.** "Use", not "leverage" or "utilize"; "is", not "serves as" or "stands
   as"; "help", not "empower". Drop "delve", "robust", "seamless", "pivotal", "crucial",
   "landscape", "journey", "tapestry" and "in today's fast-paced world".
4. **No padding.** List as many items as exist, not three because three sounds finished.
   One adjective where one is needed.
5. **No staged contrasts or drum rolls.** Not "It's not just X, it's Y", "Here's the
   thing:", "The result?" or "And that's the point." State the claim.
6. **Hedge once, where the doubt is real.** Not "may potentially" or "could possibly".
7. **Concrete over abstract.** Name the date, the number, the person, the file.
8. **Punctuation and format.** Full stops, commas and colons do the work; keep dashes rare.
   No headings, bullets or bold in an email or a message unless the owner asks, and no
   emoji they did not use first.
9. **The owner's voice wins.** When their profile note or their own messages show how they
   write, follow that over these rules.

## Before you hand it over

Read it as the recipient. Delete the first sentence if it is warm-up and the last if it
repeats the rest. Check every fact you added: nothing in the text should be something the
owner did not give you or ask you to find.

## Example

Before: "I hope this email finds you well! I wanted to reach out regarding the heating in
my apartment, which has unfortunately not been working as expected. It's worth noting that
this has been an ongoing issue. I would greatly appreciate it if you could look into this
at your earliest convenience."

After: "The heating in flat 4 has not worked since Monday. Could someone come and fix it
this week? I'm home after 5 pm, or I can leave a key with the neighbour."
